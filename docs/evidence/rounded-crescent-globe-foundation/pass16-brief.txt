## 2. WHAT A ROUNDED CRESCENT GLOBE ACTUALLY IS

A Rounded Crescent Globe is a genuine three-dimensional celestial body whose entire physical mass forms a substantial, rounded crescent.

Unlike a relatively flat crescent-shaped landmass, this geometry has significant rounded depth.

Unlike a Crescent-Cut Globe, it is not defined by removing a curved volume from an ordinary sphere.

Its physical mass naturally curves and narrows into two terminal regions.

### Fundamental physical structure

The completed World must possess:

1. A continuous crescent-shaped central structure.
2. A substantial, rounded central body.
3. A clearly convex outer profile.
4. A clearly concave inner profile.
5. Two narrowing terminal regions.
6. Genuine three-dimensional rounded mass.
7. Continuous geographical surfaces around the body.
8. Correct physical thickness and dimensions.
9. Valid closed material topology.
10. Accurate surface normals and intrinsic distances.
11. Fully authoritative numerical geography.
12. Stable geometry and geography through Save/Open.

### Intended appearance

Imagine a crescent moon, but instead of being a relatively flat cutout, the crescent itself is made of thick, rounded, globe-like material.

Its central region may be quite substantial.

The body curves around an open region, becoming narrower toward both ends.

Its cross-sections are rounded rather than broad flat slabs.

From an appropriate viewing angle, the World has a clearly recognizable crescent silhouette.

From other angles, the viewer can see its genuine rounded depth and mass.

### This World is NOT

Do not implement it as:

- A flattened crescent plate.
- A two-dimensional crescent extrusion with predominantly flat upper and lower faces.
- An ordinary Sphere with crescent lighting.
- A Sphere with a carved-out section.
- A Crescent-Cut Globe renamed.
- A Torus with a segment hidden.
- A constant-width bent Cylinder automatically labeled a crescent.
- A thin curved ribbon.
- A decorative crescent mesh.
- Multiple disconnected rounded objects.
- A rendered surface lacking genuine material volume.
- A shape whose geographical editing works only on a flat reference map.

The physical geometry must actually form the rounded crescent body.

### Distinction from the original Crescent World

The original Crescent World prioritizes a bounded crescent-shaped landmass with real material thickness, including broad geographical surfaces and appropriate physical side regions.

The Rounded Crescent Globe emphasizes a different physical structure:

- A bulbous, globe-like central mass.
- Rounded cross-sectional geometry.
- Smooth curvature around most of its exterior.
- Tapering rounded terminal regions.
- A continuous three-dimensional geographical surface.

It should not look like a flat Crescent World with increased extrusion thickness.

### Distinction from Crescent-Cut Globe

**Crescent-Cut Globe:** A spherical reference body with an actual curved material subtraction and an exposed concave cutting surface.

**Rounded Crescent Globe:** A naturally formed crescent-shaped mass whose entire physical geometry follows the crescent body.

Do not simulate the latter by applying a Boolean cut to an ordinary Sphere.

The resulting shapes may share a similar silhouette, but their physical construction and geographical surface mathematics are different.

## 3. GENUINE THREE-DIMENSIONAL ROUNDED CRESCENT GEOMETRY

Implement a coherent bounded three-dimensional solid.

A suitable mathematical construction may use a curved centerline with a variable-radius rounded cross-section.

This can create the required thick central mass and narrowing terminal regions.

Other mathematically justified constructions are acceptable if they satisfy the physical and numerical requirements.

### Suitable curved-body construction

Define a curved longitudinal centerline.

At positions along that curve, construct appropriately oriented rounded cross-sections.

Allow the cross-sectional dimensions to vary along the body's length.

The cross-section should be largest through a substantial central region and narrow toward the two ends.

### Mathematical example

A suitable general swept-surface representation may be:

`P(s, θ) = C(s) + a(s) cos(θ) N(s) + b(s) sin(θ) B(s)`

where:

- `C(s)` is the curved centerline.
- `s` is a longitudinal parameter, preferably physical arc length where practical.
- `N(s)` and `B(s)` are appropriate transverse frame vectors.
- `a(s)` describes one rounded cross-sectional radius.
- `b(s)` describes the other rounded cross-sectional radius.
- `θ` is the circumferential surface parameter.

The transverse radii may vary along the body to create a broad middle and narrower ends.

This is a candidate construction, not permission to replace an already suitable shared geometry system.

### Cross-sectional geometry

The cross-sections must produce a physically rounded body.

A circular cross-section is a reasonable baseline.

An elliptical cross-section may support additional globe-like proportions.

The geometry must not become an effectively flat slab simply because an extremely small depth parameter is convenient.

### Substantial central mass

The central region must be visibly fuller than the terminal regions.

It should resemble a thick rounded celestial body curving into a crescent.

The geometry must have genuine material volume.

A uniformly narrow curved tube is not sufficient as the only supported Crescent Globe configuration.

### Inner concave profile

The side facing the crescent opening must form a recognizable concave overall silhouette.

This must result from the physical geometry itself.

Do not generate the concavity through:

- Lighting.
- Transparency.
- A visual mask.
- A missing texture.
- A fake indentation.
- A camera-only projection trick.

### Outer convex profile

The outer boundary must create the recognizable broader crescent curve.

It must maintain correct physical geometry and surface orientation.

### Rounded material throughout

The body must be genuinely rounded through its depth.

The geographical exterior should curve continuously around its cross-section wherever the physical construction is smooth.

This is a defining difference from a crescent slab.

### Terminal regions

The Crescent Globe must narrow toward two distinct ends.

These ends should preserve a recognizable crescent silhouette while remaining physically valid.

Avoid:

- Arbitrary flat cylindrical caps.
- Missing terminal surfaces.
- Infinitely thin material tips.
- Nonmanifold convergence.
- Folded geometry.
- Invalid normals.
- Zero-area editable triangles.
- Numerical singularities exposed as terrain defects.

Smooth rounded terminal closures are preferred.

A mathematically smooth pole-like closure is acceptable if it can be represented and edited correctly.

Appropriate regularization may be used where necessary for numerical stability.

Do not make the ends so blunt that the object loses its crescent identity.

### Complete material boundary

The final World must represent one valid connected solid.

Its exterior must be complete.

There must be no unexplained missing faces, internal gaps, inverted boundaries, or decorative surfaces pretending to be physical geography.

### No internal cavity

The initial Rounded Crescent Globe is a solid body.

Its crescent opening is external empty space.

It does not require an internal hollow material chamber.

A separate Hollow Rounded Crescent Globe remains outside this assignment.

## 4. PHYSICAL CURVATURE AND SELF-INTERSECTION VALIDITY

This geometry introduces important challenges involving curved sweeps and varying body thickness.

A mathematically valid centerline does not automatically guarantee a valid physical solid.

### Local curvature constraints

The cross-sectional dimensions must remain compatible with the centerline curvature.

For a normal-offset construction, the transverse dimensions must remain inside validated limits that prevent the sweep from folding locally.

Where appropriate, use the local radius of curvature to determine supported body thickness.

### Global self-intersection

Even if every local cross-section is individually valid, different distant sections of a strongly curved Crescent may overlap.

Validate the complete physical body.

Do not allow the two terminal regions to collide unintentionally.

Do not allow the inner curve to collapse.

Do not allow cross-sections from different longitudinal positions to intersect improperly.

### Frame stability

For a planar curved centerline, use a consistent tangent-normal-binormal construction or another validated frame.

If additional supported centerline variations are implemented, preserve continuous frame orientation.

Avoid sudden flips.

Do not introduce unnecessary Ribbon-style twist editing.

### Valid body construction

Verify:

- Positive physical material thickness.
- Properly ordered cross-sections.
- Correct outward normals.
- No folded sweep surfaces.
- No invalid local Jacobians on regular charts.
- No self-intersections.
- No unexpected internal cavities.
- No disconnected physical components.
- Correct terminal closure.

### Physical clearance

The crescent opening must remain physically open.

If the body becomes too thick relative to its curvature, the opening may close or the material may intersect.

Reject those unsupported configurations.

Do not silently transform the Crescent into a Torus, Sphere, or unrelated body.

## 6. CORRECT INTRINSIC SURFACE MATHEMATICS

The Rounded Crescent Globe has genuine curved exterior geometry.

A simple flat distance calculation is not sufficient.

### Physical surface coordinates

The geometry adapter must provide:

- Accurate three-dimensional positions.
- Local tangent directions.
- Correct outward normals.
- Valid surface orientation.
- Physical distances.
- Appropriate curvature.
- Surface-area information.
- Stable chart connections.
- Valid geographical sample coordinates.
- Correct physical-to-map correspondence.

### Swept-surface metric

If the geometry uses a parameterized surface `P(s, θ)`, calculate the intrinsic metric from its actual physical derivatives.

Use:

`g_ss = ∂P/∂s · ∂P/∂s`

`g_sθ = ∂P/∂s · ∂P/∂θ`

`g_θθ = ∂P/∂θ · ∂P/∂θ`

The induced squared line element is:

`dℓ² = g_ss ds² + 2g_sθ ds dθ + g_θθ dθ²`

Do not assume the cross-term is always zero without proving that for the selected construction.

Do not assume the surface is globally equivalent to a uniform Cylinder.

The changing radius and curved centerline must be accounted for.

### Longitudinal distances

Surface distance along the crescent body depends on the actual curvature and cross-sectional shape.

An inner longitudinal route may be physically shorter than an outer route over the same parameter interval.

Brush mathematics must respect that difference.

### Circumferential distances

The exterior wraps around the rounded cross-section.

Circumferential distance changes where the cross-sectional radius changes.

Do not use one fixed circumference for the entire body.

### No shortcuts through the crescent opening

The most important requirement:

**A brush must never jump across the empty crescent opening.**

Two physically separated regions may be close together in three-dimensional distance while remaining far apart along the connected surface.

They must not be incorrectly linked.

### No shortcuts through solid material

A brush on the outer rounded surface must not pass directly through the body's volume to affect a geographically distant opposite surface.

It must travel along the real exterior.

### Correct physical connectivity

The entire body is one connected exterior surface.

Brush influence must correctly follow legitimate routes:

- Along the inner curve.
- Along the outer curve.
- Around the rounded cross-section.
- Through valid longitudinal regions.
- Toward either terminal.
- Across legitimate internal chart cuts.
- Around the terminal regions through valid surface routes.

### Terminal mathematics

Rounded terminal regions may require special numerical treatment.

If the sweep parameter becomes singular at a rounded tip, use a valid chart or canonical sample strategy.

Do not allow the terminal points to create undefined metrics, invalid normals, or corrupted numerical influence.

### Surface routing

Reuse established certified surface-distance calculations wherever mathematically valid.

If the variable-radius curved-body surface requires a specialized route method, implement a bounded and justified approach.

Avoid:

- Straight-line shortcuts across empty space.
- Shortcuts through solid material.
- Incorrect uniform-Cylinder metrics.
- Invalid flat-map approximations.
- Unbounded route enumeration.
- Excessive per-dab global computation.
- Artificial boundaries at internal charts.
- Broken terminal influence.

Numerical approximations must be validated against representative physical configurations.

Do not sacrifice creator-facing responsiveness for unnecessary exhaustive routing.
