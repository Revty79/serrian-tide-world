## 2. WHAT A CRESCENT-CUT GLOBE ACTUALLY IS

A Crescent-Cut Globe is a genuine three-dimensional spherical World from which a substantial curved portion of its material has been removed.

The remaining physical body must preserve recognizable globe-like curvature while possessing a deep curved cut that can produce a distinct crescent silhouette.

### Fundamental physical structure

The completed geometry must possess:

1. An original spherical reference body.
2. A mathematically defined curved material subtraction.
3. A remaining connected three-dimensional solid.
4. A genuine outer spherical geographical surface.
5. A real exposed concave cut surface.
6. A valid physical boundary where the two surfaces meet.
7. Meaningful remaining material volume.
8. Accurate physical dimensions.
9. Correct outward material normals.
10. Valid connected surface topology.
11. Fully authoritative numerical terrain on the remaining physical boundary.
12. Stable geometry and geography through Save/Open.

### The essential visual result

Imagine a large globe.

Another curved three-dimensional volume intersects it and removes a substantial portion of its material.

The result is a globe with a huge curved section missing.

Viewed from an appropriate angle, the remaining World resembles a crescent-shaped planet.

However, unlike our existing Crescent World, the underlying construction retains its spherical origin.

The exposed concave region must be actual physical surface geometry.

### This World is NOT

Do not implement it as:

- A Sphere with crescent lighting.
- A darkened hemisphere.
- A visual shadow.
- A crescent-shaped terrain color mask.
- A Sphere with part of its mesh merely hidden.
- A transparent section of an otherwise complete Sphere.
- A flat crescent-shaped map.
- A decorative crater texture.
- A regular Crescent World renamed.
- A Hollow Sphere with a missing outer texture.
- A carved-looking surface that cannot be sculpted.
- Two overlapping spheres rendered without a valid material subtraction.

**The missing material must actually be absent from the physical geometry.**

### Distinction from Rounded Crescent Globe

The next geometry, Rounded Crescent Globe, will be a naturally crescent-shaped, bulbous physical body with its own smooth mass distribution.

This Crescent-Cut Globe instead begins from an actual globe-like reference body and obtains its shape through a substantial curved subtraction.

These are separate physical constructions and must remain separate creator-facing World families.

## 3. GENUINE SPHERICAL MATERIAL SUBTRACTION

Implement a mathematically valid three-dimensional curved subtraction from a solid spherical reference body.

### Suitable construction

One appropriate initial model is the difference between two intersecting spherical volumes.

Let:

- `A` represent the original solid globe.
- `B` represent a second solid curved cutting volume.

The remaining World is the material belonging to `A` that is not inside `B`.

Symbolically:

`World = A \ B`

An intersecting-sphere construction is acceptable if it produces the intended recognizable crescent-cut result.

The cutting sphere may have a different radius and an offset center.

A different mathematically justified curved cutting-volume construction is acceptable if it better satisfies the required geometry and creator experience.

### Important distinction

Using a spherical cutting volume is a procedural geometry-construction technique.

It is **not** authorization to reopen our paused general excavation system.

This assignment creates a predefined World geometry from validated construction parameters.

It does not require arbitrary user-painted Boolean cutting, tunnel creation, or general material removal tools.

### Required material geometry

The completed World must represent:

- The retained exterior spherical boundary.
- The exposed surface of the cutting volume.
- Their actual physical intersection boundary.
- Correct material-side orientation.
- Complete closed exterior geometry.
- Valid connected material volume.

Do not simply render two overlapping shells and pretend the overlap represents correct subtraction.

### Real exposed concave surface

The curved cutting volume exposes a surface facing into the removed region.

That concave surface is a genuine part of the remaining World's exterior material boundary.

It must possess:

- Correct physical coordinates.
- Valid outward normals relative to the remaining material.
- Correct curvature.
- Real physical area.
- Stable surface identities.
- Authoritative terrain samples.
- Working geographical brushes.
- Correct physical visibility.
- Reliable persistence.

The exposed surface cannot be merely decorative.

### Correct normal orientation

The retained spherical exterior has outward normals consistent with the original globe.

The exposed concave cutting surface must have normals oriented outward from the remaining solid, which generally means toward the removed volume.

Do not blindly reuse the cutting sphere's normal orientation without accounting for the subtraction.

Incorrect normal orientation can cause rendering, lighting, picking, and geographical displacement-direction problems.

### Intersection boundary

Where the retained globe surface and the exposed cut surface meet, they form an actual physical intersection.

For an intersecting-sphere model, the intersection is typically a circle.

That boundary must be represented correctly.

Requirements include:

- Accurate physical position.
- Stable topology.
- Correct adjacency.
- No artificial gap.
- No overlapping material shells.
- Valid numerical sample ownership.
- Correct terrain continuity.
- Proper physical brush crossing.
- Appropriate rendering of the real surface crease.

A genuine physical crease may have different surface normals on either side.

Do not incorrectly smooth those normals into a nonexistent continuous tangent surface.

Conversely, do not introduce an artificial gap between the connected surfaces.

### Closed material boundary

The resulting World must be one valid connected solid.

The material boundary must be complete.

The cut must not leave missing mesh patches, inverted material, nonmanifold edges, or disconnected fragments.

### No invalid internal cavities

The initial Crescent-Cut Globe represents an exposed curved subtraction.

Do not accidentally generate a fully enclosed hidden cavity inside the original Sphere.

That would introduce a different internal material-boundary problem.

The removed region must intersect the exterior in the intended way.

### No unintended tunnels

The subtraction must not automatically produce a through-tunnel or multiple unrelated openings.

A genuine Crescent-Cut Globe is the objective.

Tunnel construction belongs to the later excavation system.

## 5. CORRECT PHYSICAL SURFACE TOPOLOGY AND MATHEMATICS

The Crescent-Cut Globe combines at least two differently oriented curved physical surface regions.

Its brushes must use their actual geometry.

### Retained globe surface

The retained original spherical exterior must preserve appropriate spherical surface mathematics.

Reuse established Sphere geometry and intrinsic distance calculations where valid.

However, the removed region must no longer be treated as traversable spherical geography.

### Exposed cut surface

The concave cut requires its own correct physical metric and orientation.

If the cutting surface is spherical, use its actual radius and spherical geometry.

Do not assume the cut shares the original globe's center or radius.

### Correct shared boundary

The two curved surfaces connect along their actual intersection.

That boundary must be recognized by the physical surface-routing system.

A brush crossing it must follow the connected surface correctly.

### No shortcuts through missing material

The most important rule is:

**A brush must never travel through the removed spherical volume as though that material still exists.**

If two remaining geographical regions are physically close across the cut, their connected surface distance may still be substantially longer.

The brush must respect that actual surface route.

### No shortcuts through solid material

Likewise, a brush cannot travel directly through the surviving globe's solid interior.

The correct influence follows the exterior material boundary.

### Surface-route behavior

Support genuine connected routes:

- Along the retained globe exterior.
- Along the concave cut.
- Across the real intersection boundary.
- Around the cut's rim.
- Between surface regions through legitimate exterior connections.

### Surface crease mathematics

The cut boundary may be a real geometric crease.

A path crossing that crease must account for the true surface geometry on both sides.

Do not pretend the original sphere and cutting sphere form one smooth spherical surface.

Do not treat the crease as an impassable boundary.

### Spherical patch routing

Reuse existing certified spherical route mechanisms where valid.

A complete-sphere geodesic that crosses the removed portion of the original globe is not a valid route on the Crescent-Cut Globe.

Similarly, a great-circle calculation over the complete cutting sphere cannot automatically use cut-surface regions that do not belong to the remaining World.

The routing method must be restricted to the legitimate retained surface patches and their actual connections.

### Resource-bounded calculation

Develop or reuse a scalable connected-surface distance method.

Avoid:

- Unbounded exhaustive face-route enumeration.
- Full global recomputation per dab.
- Invalid straight-line shortcuts.
- False connections through the removed volume.
- Artificial brush barriers at the intersection.
- Incorrect pole handling.
- Unbounded memory growth.

If an approximate route method is used, justify and verify its error bounds.

The method must remain suitable for normal browser terrain painting.
