## Physical construction and connected distance

The supported family is the solid difference of two spheres of **equal radius R**, with centers 0 and d times a unit axis. Creators choose R from 10,000 to 1,000,000,000 m, d/R from 0.25 to 1.5, longitude and latitude. Globe and cut curvature radii are explicitly linked. Cut depth is 2R-d; the intersection circle lies in the plane p.axis=d/2 and has radius sqrt(R squared - d squared/4). The default d/R=0.6 retains 43.65% of the original globe volume.

Every supported configuration has one connected positive-volume body, a retained spherical exterior and a real exposed concave spherical cap, joined at one circular crease. There is no hidden cavity, unintended tunnel, overlapping shell or inactive original-sphere geography.

For an intrinsic point p on the radius-R sphere, keep p when p.axis <= d/2. Otherwise reflect it across the plane p.axis=d/2. The reflected cap belongs to the cutter sphere and lies inside the original globe. Its inverse is the same reflection; the retained patch is unchanged. This continuous bijection fixes the circle and maps onto the complete boundary of A minus B. The physical topology is a sphere: two disks joined along their shared circle.

This particular construction is a global **length isometry**. The derivative on each open patch is orthogonal. Curve speed is therefore preserved almost everywhere; on any rim interval the axial derivative vanishes and the mapping is identity. The inverse preserves length too. Taking infima over corresponding curves proves that connected physical surface distance equals great-circle distance in the intrinsic sphere. Folded geodesics follow actual retained/cut geography, including rim crossings. This proof is specific to the equal-curvature family. It does not assume ordinary spherical distance for arbitrary sphere subtraction. At d/R=0.25, opposing banks separated by a 0.25R chord can still require pi R along the boundary.

The intrinsic numerical provider is the accepted equiangular cube-sphere lattice, explicitly nested inside `crescent-cut-globe-world` physical metadata. Every intrinsic coordinate maps to active physical geography. It is not an ordinary Sphere World. The analytical rim may cross a numerical cell without duplicating its scalar field or introducing independently writable aliases. On the cut, the outward material normal is minus the reflected intrinsic normal; the tangent gradient is reflected without that minus sign. The real crease retains its two physical normals.

The reflection and curve-length proof above is our derivation. Supporting primary references describe [planar reflection as a rigid motion](https://repository.gatech.edu/bitstreams/72b03ecc-0c98-461c-8aba-8f81939bf85c/download), [orthogonal spherical isometries](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~oleg/math/papers-html/rel-refl/article.html), [sphere-plane intersection circles](https://www.math.utah.edu/online/2210/Practice_Problems/Probs4/Probs4ans.pdf), and [ray-sphere intersections](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/spring20/cos426/precepts/Precept-6.pdf). These references do not independently certify the complete application construction.

## Limits and owner review

Globe and cutter curvature radii are linked. Independently sized cutters need a different certified intrinsic solver. Terrain uses the accepted numerical height/gradient presentation on the reference boundary; this pass does not add volumetric terrain displacement. Opaque projections can appear cap-like; the physical rim, concave terrain and rotation show the curved subtraction. The shape is a solid curved body rather than a planar crescent prism.

Owner review should assess the default presentation, curved terrain/coast sharpness, both banks and rim, configuration/orientation controls, maps, ordinary painting and navigation. General excavation, arbitrary Boolean cuts, free collision navigation, links/tunnels, persistent Undo, host integration and deployment remain outside this pass.
