## Physical geography and inherited mechanisms

The ordinary Atlas selector creates a regular tetrahedron with configurable
physical edge length. Four genuine equilateral triangular faces, including the
base, share six real edges and four vertices. One indexed Float32 authority
stores exactly 32,770 words at N128 or 131,074 at N256. Geometry metadata contains
the actual regular-tetrahedron provider and edge dimension, with no square-base
or deformed-pyramid encoding.

The existing connected dab/stroke executor, conservative candidates, certified
unfolded paths, falloff, smoothing and exact whole-gesture history remain in use.
`createPolyhedronBrush` is shared by Pyramid and Tetrahedron. The worker uses the
accepted warm-worker runtime, private bulk source epochs, prepared-patch protocol
and host-authoritative atomic commit. Both drawing and exact hover preparation
use the existing scheduler. Rolling work epochs preserve one held gesture without
raising per-dab, queue or history limits.

Physical editing can cross all six visible connected edges, including triangular
base joins. The connected Surface map unfolds three side triangles around the
central triangular base. Attached map edges are continuous; separated net edges
remain honest map cuts. The shared Atlas input/workspace handles drawing,
navigation, interruption, history, menus and persistence. It does not invent
connections between physically separate boundaries.

## Strict Save/Open and continuation

Format 17 records the actual regular geometry, identity, triangular ownership,
sample count, finite physical elevation bound and canonical little-endian Float32
payload. Complete field validation and checksum verification precede adoption.
Unknown fields, foreign identities, alternate providers, wrong counts, corrupt
words and unsupported detail reject. Existing formats 1 through 16 retain their
interpretation and replacement guards.

Cancellation and stale source revisions retain active geography. Save/reopen
preserves every canonical word, including signed zero. Native browser workflows
then sculpt the reopened world with a fresh worker and verify exact Undo/Redo
back to the saved geography, followed by exact Redo of that continued edit.

The new provider also rejects custom elevation bounds above its physical limit;
worker metadata cannot enlarge that limit. Ordinary bounds and earlier foundation
brush results remain unchanged.
