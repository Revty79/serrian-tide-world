A6-E now adds browser-local v3 Save/Open through the separate `SphericalEditor` host, specified in [SPHERICAL-PERSISTENCE.md](SPHERICAL-PERSISTENCE.md). D2's session-only statements below describe its original checkpoint/test host. The four tools, physical previews, net assistance and numerical algorithms remain intact. Current stopping point: E; the next pass is not authorized.

This extends the [D1 projection foundation](SPHERICAL-VIEWS.md) and [A6-B/C numerical tools](SPHERICAL-SCULPTING.md). The application offers a blank editable sphere separately from the preserved read-only demonstration. Radius must be explicitly supplied in meters; N=128/256 are development resolutions. Blank elevation is −100 m, sea level 0. Temporary UUIDs satisfy terrain identity contracts without creating World records.

## One authority and replaceable host

`SphericalPreview` creates a disposable provider and derived cache. Blank initialization skips the deterministic fixture. Texture preparation yields between faces. `SphericalSession` wraps the original provider's validated canonical write method to record changed indices; its `terrain` and the viewport both refer to that original authority. The wrapper delegates every read, write, identity, alias and revision. There is no additional elevation field for either view. Private history snapshots and RGBA display pixels are copies with specific purposes, not alternative writable geography.

`SphericalViewport` accepts a provider/cache and optional session. Without a session it retains D1's read-only navigation. With a session, `spherical-editing.ts` arbitrates browser input, while the session owns queue/history/cache updates. Numerical tools, geometry, projected preview math and session/controller state do not import React, database or browser storage. A future Next.js client host can replace the development shell and supply its authorized World/surface provider; authentication, permissions, World records and persistence remain host responsibilities. No original-site integration is performed.

The prior planar/wrapped document, canvas/camera and history remain mounted in an inert subtree. Spherical keyboard actions do not reach that editor. Local Save/Open is never called with sphere data. Runtime tests explicitly prove all three v1/v2 encoder entry points reject a spherical provider. The formats and IndexedDB adapter are unchanged.

## Physical brushes and visible domain

Pointer positions pass through D1's inverse projection to unit physical directions with matching World/surface identity. The existing engine retains real geodesic distances, cubic falloff, Float32 saturation, immutable pre-dab source evaluation, radius/4 great-circle spacing and its near-antipodal/work-limit guards. Each session freezes settings at stroke start.

The GUI exposes radius in meters (0.005R–0.2R, default 0.08R), positive Raise/Lower m/dab, Smooth/Flatten percentage intensity and signed meter Flatten target. Blank/invalid drafts disable painting. These development UI limits bound per-dab work; the numerical API retains its existing 1e−12…π angular radius range. No latitude, chart or screen-unit substitution occurs.

For the globe, an optional physical hemisphere domain is frozen to the current camera forward vector for the duration of the stroke. Only samples with nonnegative direction dot forward can be written. The influence is the geodesic cap intersected with the visible hemisphere, preventing hidden-side edits at the limb. Smooth can read connected neighbors across that boundary while preparing visible samples, but cannot write hidden ones. A net stroke uses the complete cap. Away from the limb both views apply the identical footprint; at the limb the explicit visibility clipping is intentional and shown in the preview. Rotate/recenter before precision work there because orthographic inversion is ill-conditioned.

`spherical-brush-preview.ts` constructs a true small circle `c cos α + (a cos t + b sin t) sin α`, α=radius/R. Analytic roots split it at chart boundary planes and the globe horizon before display sampling. Net cuts receive separate cap arcs and cut-edge closure segments; the globe receives visible cap arcs plus any inside-cap horizon arc. No straight connector crosses a net gap. The SVG is pointer-transparent and shares the actual inverse/view/size with painting. Tests compare its physical containment to every affected canonical sample for faces, twelve edges, eight corners, poles and a grazing limb, as well as checking boundary distances/finite projections. Only the final display polyline is sampled (up to roughly 128 subdivisions per full circle).

## Gesture policy and assisted drawing

Brush and Rotate/Pan modes are explicit. Mouse/pen primary contact paints; middle mouse or Space navigates. One touch waits for movement over 5 CSS pixels or release before its first dab. A second contact claims two-finger navigation; the remaining contact stays in navigation until all contacts lift. A simultaneous two-finger start makes no initial brush dab. A second contact arriving after intentional single-finger drawing ends the applied portion as one operation, then navigates.

Release drains all accepted queued physical segments and commits exactly one history operation. Escape, pointercancel, lost capture, blur, visibility/resize, tool/settings changes, view actions or entering overlaid controls terminate safely. Cancellation retains the already applied portion as one operation and discards unprocessed input. It does not claim to replay a canceled tail. Zero-effect strokes preserve Redo. Undo/Redo and camera/view changes do not resample or relocate geography.

`continuousNetSegment` partitions the entire screen segment at tile boundaries. Every traversed interval must contain real terrain; each face transition must be a displayed edge join whose physical directions agree. Empty gaps, disconnected cuts and ambiguous diagonal corner shortcuts stop the gesture. Lift and restart on the desired face, or select/recenter it on the globe. A brush cap still affects all physically adjacent samples across numerical edges/corners, even where their display occurrences are cut apart.

Net screen-edge assistance defaults on and can be disabled. During painting on real terrain, a 200 ms dwell in the A5.1 48 CSS-pixel edge band pans the net, capped at 180 CSS px/s with 32 ms time steps. The prior screen anchor moves with the view before the stationary pointer is inverted again; this preserves physical spacing. Assistance waits while numerical input is backlogged. It stops at invalid terrain/cuts and finite pan limits; it never repeats a spherical rectangle.

**Specific limitation, isolated correction A6-D2-R:** automatic globe rotation/recentering during a held limb stroke is not implemented. Lift, rotate/recenter and resume. Safely adding that gesture requires a moving visibility domain, physical anchor/camera transport, matching previews and exact spacing/history proofs. It must not paint the far side or reuse periodic scrolling. This is explicitly disclosed in the UI and delivery; it is not claimed complete and no follow-up is begun here.

## Scheduling, geometry cache and incremental display

`moveToSteps` yields after each complete numerical dab. The synchronous API consumes the same generator, preserving expression order and bits. The browser processes at most eight complete dabs per frame: nominally 6 ms for ordinary input, increasing to 16 ms when more than one position is queued or release is draining a stroke. This catches up accepted work without dropping segments or residual spacing. A single dab/history snapshot can exceed the budget; it cannot be interrupted midway without changing pre-dab semantics. The queue is limited to 2,048 positions; overflow stops safely with a visible message. Released backlogs show **Finishing numerical stroke…**; they are not hidden behind a fake completed animation.

Smooth caches immutable neighbor indices, inverse-geodesic-square weights and totals in a per-provider WeakMap/LRU, capped at 8,192 stencils. It rereads current elevations on every dab. Ordered calculations match the old formula exactly. A cache payload is at most 393,216 bytes (typically less at corners), plus JS bookkeeping. The complete old 272-dab N=128/256 benchmarks retain their exact edited SHA256 and Undo/Redo hashes. See the [new numerical receipt](evidence/a6-d2-numerical-performance.json).

Tracked writes accumulate canonical indices. On revision change, `spherical-texture-updates.ts` expands every changed sample's aliases by its bilinear support, unions a dirty rectangle per affected chart, sparsely reads required elevations and updates only those RGBA pixels. Canvas2D uploads the same dirty rectangles. Undo/Redo uses the same tracked writes. An untracked external revision triggers a conservative full display rebuild; ordinary editing tests require zero such rebuilds. Patching is tested byte-for-byte against complete fresh textures after all edge/corner edits and history restoration.

Editable sessions retain six canonical index lattices (not heights) to avoid rebuilding address resolution on each patch. RGBA + lattice typed payloads total 1,984,560 bytes at N=128 and 7,901,232 at N=256, in addition to authority 393,224 / 1,572,872 bytes. Six Canvas2D images, the visible backing canvas (≤3M pixels/DPR≤1.5), scratch raster, JS objects, transient patches, geometry stencils and history are additional. History retains at most 16 MiB/100 entries; its pending/commit snapshots and dab preparation remain explicit transient costs. This accounting is not process RSS.

Globe raster budgets during painting are 80,000 desktop / 35,000 coarse-pointer pixels, restoring 420,000 / 220,000 after commit. Navigation retains D1's 180,000 / 70,000 and delayed refinement. Actual edits are presented each frame through the updated cache. Hover-only preview/readout changes do not repaint terrain. Net views reuse patched texture canvases. Display interpolation creates no higher-resolution geographic detail; genuinely higher-resolution/tiled authority and GPU/worker rendering require later design.

Latency instrumentation records each input segment until its final numerical dab has reached the displayed cache/raster, including queue delay; navigation latency is separate. Bounded arrays retain the latest 120 observations. End-to-end test wall time additionally includes native event dispatch, history commit and test waits. CPU-throttled Pixel 7 emulation is evidence from this Windows host, not a physical-device or universal frame-rate claim. The [completion report](A6-D2-COMPLETION.md) gives measured results and limits.

## Session protection and boundary

Modified state follows spherical history state IDs plus an active changed stroke. Undo to the original blank state clears it; Redo restores it. Return asks before discarding modified sphere data. beforeunload uses the browser's standard warning after ending an active gesture. Browsers may suppress warnings or omit unload on device/process termination. There is no autosave, recovery or sphere persistence in D2. Never depend on this session as a saved document.
